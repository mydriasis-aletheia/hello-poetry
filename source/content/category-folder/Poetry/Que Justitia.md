Some iteration may follow.
*Lex injusta non est lex.* (An unjust law is not a law.)
*Rex injusta non est rex.* (An unjust king is not a king.)
*Deus injusta non est deus.* (An unjust god is not a god.)
Here there is an appeal to purity, to negate an authority
by reviewing its coherence with the moral order.
Justice becomes justice *for* some person or entity
and never justice of its own sake.

Some recursion may follow.
*Justitia relativus non est justitia.* (Relative justice is not justice).
Here there is an appeal to universality, to negate a judgement
by reviewing its validity with the logical concept.
Justice must be universalizable if it is to be justice 
of its own sake, that is, impartial, fair and equitable.
